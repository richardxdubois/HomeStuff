We visited Puglia - the heel of the Italian boot - to kayak for a week at the end of September on the Adriatic and Ionian seas organized by Tofino Expeditions, executed by Travel in Kayak; Marco Venturini and Emmanuele Mandoloni were our most excellent guides.

Not only did the trip involve kayaking both coasts and seeing the remarkable cliffs and caves, but also being based in two unique Masserias (boutique hotels) and sampling superb Italian cuisine. Each dinner was special - generally in a small town with the menu selected by Marco.

We flew into Bari via Munich, and stayed at an airport hotel the first night. Then we took the train to Lecce where we were to meet the group; we had an afternoon to explore the old town of Lecce. Similarly after the trip, we made our way back to Bari where we met Fermi telescope colleagues and had a great catch-up and lunch. Then back to the same airport hotel to get ready for the 6am flight back to Munich.

Kayaking

It had been windy when we arrived and water conditions on the Adriatic side were too rough. But the trip is designed to be flexible - if it's too windy on one coast, go to the other one. Which we did. It was a 40' drive to the Ionian coast where we had our initial paddle, heading south. All the group were in tandems, most in Delta Kayak 17.5s. As is our habit, Deb took the rear on our boat, allowing me to take photos from the front. In general, we were in good sync, except for the occasions where I would skip a stroke or be unable to resist "helping" steer.

Wind conditions persisted, so we stayed on the Ionian side for the next two outings, both out of Santa Maria di Leuca, at the bottom of the heel. We went west the first day, and east the second, with more significant swells on the east (Adriatic) side, probably 3' (we said 6).

The winds had been steadily dropping during the week, so our next paddle was north of Otranto. Here we got to experience a small surf launch and then landing. The swells were smaller and after the previous day, we just shrugged them off.

That was it for kayaking on the trip. The 2nd last day was essentially a rest day, and the final day's water conditions were not conducive to paddling. Marco found things for us to do in lieu of paddling, notably a visit to the Egnazia Roman ruins and museum near Fasano. The ruins are a bit uninspiring, but the museum was excellent.

The Masserias

We were based out of two Masserias: one in the south in the Salento region - Masseria Violante, between Ruffano and Specchia; and the other in the north in the Valle d'Itria region - Trullo Barbagiullo, near Cisternino.

Masseria Violante is an updated 17th century "hub" for the families supporting a local landowner's agriculture - probably about 5 families. They kept the style of the place in the remodeling, built around a courtyard, with dry-stone walls and century-old olive trees on the grounds. The pool is new. It is owned and operated by a small family, with 2 dogs and a cat (who likes to sneak into the rooms).

Trullo Barbagiullo is in the agricultural countryside near Cisternino and its main suite is a trullo - a stone yurt: dry walled, conical and unique to this part of Puglia. We lucked out and got the main suite; we slept under the conical roof. It was made for short people.

We had breakfasts at both locales. They put on excellent spreads, with everything made/baked on site. It was a good thing we were burning off calories paddling. Ditto for the suppers.

The Towns

We had brief looks at Lecce (on arrival), Otranto, Cisternino, Alberobello and Bari (on departure), with the middle three being with the group. Alberobello is different in that it is a haven for Trulli, and famous for them. The other two are old, small towns where we concentrated on the storico centro. Otranto featured a castle.

Cameras

The workhorses for this trip were the waterproof Olympus TG4 and iPhone 14 Pro. Sadly, my beloved Canon SX70 got damp on the 2nd day in the kayak and shorted out, never to revive. I used the Canon SX740 as backup on dry land. As the SX70 has been discontinued, a Nikon P950 is on its way as a replacement.

Impressions

The reason for the trip was kayaking, with a subtitle of gastronomy. We were in a group of 8, and once again we were fortunate to have a fun group. It was split between Canadians and Americans; 6 of 8 from the West Coast. Late September proved to be a good time to go - the weather was excellent, but most of the (other) tourists had left. I'm always surprised that for the entire trip, we were north of San Francisco's latitude. Our guides were everything we could ask for - skilled in a kayak; patient with all of us and super helpful. They did a great job of reacting to conditions and remaking the plan when needed. The kayaks were top-notch (Deltas) and they supplied carbon fibre paddles. I had had no real expectations for what we would see and was very pleasantly surprised by the towering cliffs and caves in the south. And the ultra clear, ultra blue water. We had some waves and swells to deal with, but the boats (and we) were up to it.

It was essentially a cashless trip - all except for the guides' tips, everything went on Apple Pay. Handy and more secure.

The food was excellent - Marco picked great out-of-the way restaurants, was known to the owners and did all the ordering for us (ok, maybe not desserts). Typically once we'd made it through the shared starters, we were full up. Even still, we did not feel stuffed; the food was not heavy. I've already mentioned the fine breakfasts. Lunches were more utilitarian and were split between picnic lunches between paddling and a couple of restaurant lunches - both hit the spot.

Wandering the old towns was also a treat. Marco would typically take us to the center, tell us a little of the town and set us free for 60-90 minutes or so to wander on our own. And challenge us to find our way back to the starting point. I cheated and dropped pins in Google maps. Alberobello was the only real tourist hot spot, and we arrived late in the afternoon when the flagged tours were leaving. It seems like a fairy tale place with all the Trulli.

We also made sure to load up on gelato, especially at Martinucci's in Santa Maria di Leuca, said to be the best in the region. Their Fondante was very intense dark chocolate.

We were reminded again what nice people Italians are! One random example is when I was struggling a bit to roll our tandem up a sandy incline at the end of the day, a beachgoer just picked up the back end of the boat and got me over the hump. I very much enjoyed my last-day reunion with my Fermi colleagues from Bari - great to catch up and treated to an excellent lunch!

It was a little disappointing we could not be on the water the last day, but you can't control the weather. The trip should have been 1-2 days longer, to accommodate weather issues. Apparently last year's version was in fact 2 days longer!

All in all, another great kayaking trip - fun group, super guides, excellent equipment, along with a bit of local culture and superb food. What's not to like.
