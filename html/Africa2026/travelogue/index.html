We traveled through Tanzania, Kenya, Zimbabwe, and Botswana from 14 August to 4 September 2026 — four countries and, in a real sense, two different kinds of safari.

[DRAFT] Tanzania and Kenya gave us abundance: the Ngorongoro crater's density, the Serengeti–Mara migration, Tarangire's dry-season elephant herds. Botswana was a different lens entirely — a landscape too dry to support that kind of volume, where everything worth seeing sits close to the one river or delta channel forcing water to a single place. Same animals, in places, seen for very different reasons.

[DRAFT] Each section below covers a different slice of the trip: the animals and birds themselves (the main point of going), a standalone collection of scenery and aerial photography, the texture of the safari experience day to day, and Victoria Falls on its own, since it felt like its own trip entirely.
