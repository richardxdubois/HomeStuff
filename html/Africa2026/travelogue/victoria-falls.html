[DRAFT] Victoria Falls felt like its own trip, dropped in the middle of the safari — different enough in kind (geology and a river, not wildlife) that it earns a standalone page rather than living inside Scenery & Aerials.

The Victoria Falls Hotel itself was enough to make this segment of the trip stand on its own. It's no bush camp! And we could go outside in the evenings. The main experiences here were to see the famous Falls and Zambezi river, and to experience the Victoria Falls Hotel.

We immediately got the feel of the hotel when they told us they have 3 restaurants, and one of them requires elegant attire. We just laughed. With our 15 kg total luggage weight limits, we barely had enough clothes at all, let alone fancy ones.

That said, the hotel was built in 1904 as English colonial, and they've maintained the vibe admirably. Down to pith helmets on the uniformed porters. It's a sprawling, classy joint. Our meals were on the Stanley Terrace with the other hoi polloi - breakfasts feature the sun rising directly over the Zambezi gorge. It was fun getting the dinner servers to recommend Zimbabwean fare and explain why. We were told that warthogs "mow" the hotel lawns, only to find that information dated - now it's done by Huskvarna "Roombas".

Our reservation came with a complimentary massage - for once, we decided to take them up on it. I could get to like massages! I went for back and neck; Deb for foot and leg.

After our first lunch on the Terrace, we walked to the Victoria Falls Bridge for our first look at the Falls. Not quite realizing the geometry of them and that the one narrow waterfall we could see was because of a T in the narrow gorge. The walk wasn't particularly pleasant, but the view from the bridge was great. The full scope of the falls and the river feeding them came from the short helicopter tour.

The falls are something like 1.7 km long. The falls are 1000' high, as is width of the gorge. You can't really see that from the rim of the gorge. We got the close up view from the Zambian side of the falls. The dry season is not peak water levels, but the plus is that in high water, there is so much spray in the narrow gorge that you can't see anything but spray, and your camera would drown.

Passport control for both the Zambian and Zimbabwean sides were zoo-ey, but our guide minimized the hassle (and potential mistakes).

We took a sunset cruise on the Zambezi, not far from the Falls. You certinly could see the spray. It was pretty relaxed and we got to see some wildlife on the islands, mostly crocodiles and birds. We were treated to some hippos playing in the water as well.

We went with Zambezi helicopters, and they provided a well run tour. They're short, but you see it all. I lucked out when they put me in the co-pilot's seat, which was great for picture taking. The camera on its Vortex harness on my chest may have helped them decide. The 13' ride featured 3 loop-de-loops and then return to base. The Victoria Falls Hotel is under the flight path, so we had a great overview of it too. Zambezi (for a modest fee) provided photos they had taken of us boarding the helicopter, as well as a partly generic video of the journey.

We had planned on checking out the markets at Elephants Walk, but, ummm, took a wrong and ended up somewhere definitely not the market we expected. Not going to say more about that, other than we contributed to the local economy and were surely taken.
