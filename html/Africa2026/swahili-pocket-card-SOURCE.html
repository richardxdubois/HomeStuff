Kiswahili · Tanzania & Kenya

Swahili & Setswana

Enough to be greeted well, thank people properly, and name what you are looking at.

Saying it

Swahili is spelled as it sounds — five clean vowels, no silent letters, no reduction. a as in father, e as in bet, i as in machine, o as in or, u as in flute. Stress falls on the second-to-last syllable, bar a few Arabic loanwords. ny is the ny of canyon; ng’ is a single nasal sound, as in singer.

Greeting is a ritual

You do not walk up and ask for something. There is an exchange first, and it is short, formulaic, and expected. Skipping it reads as rudeness; doing it badly does not.

The one that earns you goodwill

To anyone visibly older than you, say Shikamoo (shee-ka-MOH-oh). It is a mark of respect — literally “I hold your feet.” The reply is Marahaba. Children use it to adults, adults to elders. As a grey-haired visitor you will be on the receiving end of it too, so know the answer.

About “Jambo”

Camp staff will greet you with Jambo! It is real but simplified — the version reserved for tourists. Answering Sijambo, or better Habari?, signals you have made some effort. It is reliably well received.

Two words you will hear constantly

Safari is simply the Swahili for journey — no drama attached. And Twende! means “let’s go”; it is how every game drive begins.

When you hand over a tip

Say it, don’t just pass it. Asante sana kwa kila kitu — with eye contact, and either two hands or the right hand with the left touching the forearm. The gesture registers more than the amount does.

Botswana · Setswana

Swahili is no use here — it is an East African language. Botswana speaks Setswana and English. Camp English is universal, but rra and mma cost nothing and are noticed. Setswana g is a throaty kh, as in loch — never an English h.

Pula is both the currency and the word for rain — in a country that is mostly Kalahari the two ideas are not separable, and it doubles as a toast. Animals: tlou elephant, tau lion, thutlwa giraffe, kubu hippo, nkwe leopard.

Also worth having, in Swahili: leo today, kesho tomorrow, asubuhi morning, jioni evening, mchana afternoon, usiku night, adhuhuri noon, sasa now, baadaye later, karibu na near to, mbali na far from, vumbi dust, nimechoka I’m tired.
